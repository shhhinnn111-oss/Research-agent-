## A. VIOLENCE DATA (verify against SATP/CRSS/PIPS/GTI)

### CRSS (Center for Research and Security Studies)
- 2024: 2,555 violence-linked fatalities nationally (2,546 in another release), 1,981 injured.
- 2025 (Annual Report, 31 Dec 2025): 1,272 violence incidents; 3,417 fatalities; 2,134 injured. +34% YoY. Deadliest year in a decade (highest since 2015).
  - KP: 2,331 fatalities (+44% YoY; +711 deaths; 82% of net national rise); 795 incidents (63%).
  - Balochistan: 956 fatalities (28%); 386 incidents (30%).
  - KP+Balochistan = 96% of fatalities, 93% of incidents.
  - Outlaws: 2,060 killed (60% of all fatalities, highest since 2015) in 392 security ops; +120% vs 2024.
  - Terrorist attacks: ~880, killing 1,357 civilians + security personnel.
  - Army/FC: 374 killed incl. 22 high-ranking officers (1 Lt Col, 16 Majors, 4 Captains, 2 Lieutenants); 341 lower-ranking soldiers killed; 400+ wounded.
  - Border closure with Afghanistan from 11 Oct 2025 → brief decline: by December attacks fell 17%, civilian/SF fatalities -19%.
  - AJK: 15 fatalities in 2025 vs 0 in 2024.
- Q1 2026: 813 deaths national; KP down >57% vs Q4 2025; 18 drone attacks nationally (16 in KP, 1 Balochistan, 1 Punjab — one hit Islamabad imambargah during Friday prayers killing 36; formally claimed: 1, TTP strike on FC Fort Karak).
- Q2 2026: 774 fatalities, 336 injured, 267 incidents. KP 475 (+53% vs Q1's 311, i.e. KP share 61% of national fatalities); Balochistan 265 (34%). 63% of KP incidents in Bannu, Lakki Marwat, Kurram, Bajaur. Civilian fatalities 187; security personnel 134; outlaws 453 in ~80 ops. Terrorist attacks outnumbered security ops ~2.3:1 (188 vs 79). Claims: 22 fatal incidents claimed (76 deaths); BLA 8 attacks/41 deaths; TTP 7 attacks/26 deaths.
- Q3 2026: 385 incidents; 1,415 fatalities; 558 injuries. +83% vs Q2. Balochistan 829 (59%) — surpassed KP for first time as most affected; KP 556 (39% of fatalities but 67% of injuries). Militant fatalities +103% (453→921) — 3/4 of national increase.
- 2024→2025: 34% surge; 2024 had sharpest jump at 67% vs 2023.

- 4 Mar 2026: TTP-ISKP clash in Ghaljo Narai, Orakzai — ISKP commander Haji Rehman (aka Haji Dada) killed + 3 associates.

- 2026 TTP-linked (to 23 Aug 2026): ≥128 incidents, ≥559 killed (53 civilians, 81 SF, 425 militants).

## B. AFGHANISTAN–PAKISTAN CONFLICT (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026)
- Oct 2025: major cross-border clashes; Qatar-mediated ceasefire in Doha (Oct 2025); Istanbul talks (Nov 2025) agreed monitoring/verification mechanism; no durable settlement. (khaama.com, 3 Oct 2026)
- 21 Feb 2026: war begins (border clashes). 26 Feb 2026: Pakistan launches Operation Ghazab-lil-Haq ("Righteous Fury"). Defence Minister Khawaja Asif: "open war."
- Pakistani claims: 583 Afghan Taliban killed, 795 injured; 242 checkposts destroyed (Info Minister Tarar, 7 Mar 2026); earlier: 133 killed in first strikes on Kabul, Paktia, Kandahar; 27 posts destroyed, 9 captured; 2 corps HQ, 3 brigade HQ, 2 ammo depots, 1 logistics base, 3 bn HQ, 2 sector HQ, 80+ tanks/artillery/APCs destroyed; 274 Taliban killed for 12 Pakistani troops (ISPR DG Lt Gen Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry, 27 Feb 2026).
- Afghan claims: 55 Pakistani soldiers killed, 110 killed (later), 27 posts captured; denied by Pakistan.
- Ceasefires: Eid al-Fitr (19–24 Mar 2026, at Saudi/Qatar/Turkiye request); Eid al-Adha (TTP announced 3-day, 24 May 2026). Fighting resumed; low-intensity through summer.
- Sept 2026: escalation after mass-casualty attack in Kohat; 24 Sep strikes on 10 sites in Kandahar, Paktia, Khost (4 civilians killed per Afghan authorities); 1 Oct 2026 strikes on Kunar and Helmand (UNAMA: ≥10 civilians killed, mostly children; 9 injured, 3 homes destroyed).
- UNAMA: 764 civilian casualties between 26 Jan–31 Mar 2026 (372 killed, 392 injured); 1,100+ civilian deaths/injuries in Afghanistan in 2026 (by late Sep).
- Mediators: Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Türkiye, China (Kabul-Islamabad talks in China, Apr 2026 → ceasefire commitment in Nari, Kamdesh, Bargi Matal, Arandu, Chitral areas).
- Reported: Pakistan side claims terror incidents in KP fell 65% after operation start (240 → 80) — official claim, treat cautiously.
- Oct 2025-Nov 2025: Pakistan carried air/drone strikes in Afghanistan (Kabul, Kandahar, Kunar, Khost, Nangarhar, Paktia, Paktika) between Oct 2025 and Sept 2026.

## G. PTM
- PTM banned 6 Oct 2024 under Section 11B of Anti-Terrorism Act 1997 (First Schedule), citing "activities prejudicial to peace and security"; notification by Interior Ministry (Section Officer Umar Hayat Chattha).
- Founded May 2014 as Mahsud Tahafuz Movement (de-mining); renamed PTM Jan 2018.
- Demands: de-mining, freedom of movement, end to extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances, unlawful detentions; truth and reconciliation framework.
- KP government (Barrister Saif) announced strict enforcement 10 Oct 2024; Section 144 imposed on Khyber district gathering; clashes between police and PTM supporters (9 Oct 2024).
- CM Gandapur met Manzoor Pashteen late Sep 2024 — tension with federal ban; Balochistan had imposed 90-day ban on Pashteen entry (Aug 2024).
- Pashtun Gaumi Jirga (grand Pashtun jirga) was scheduled 11 Oct 2024.
- Ali Wazir (ex-MNA, South Waziristan) repeatedly jailed.

## H. FATA MERGER / MERGED DISTRICTS (NMDs)
- FATA: 27,220 sq km; ~4.8–5.0m population (2017 census: 5.0m); 7 agencies + 6 frontier regions. Merged via 25th Amendment (May 2018); 23 KP Assembly seats added (KP NA seats 43 → 55 with 12 NA seats).
- FATA Reforms Committee (2016, chaired by Sartaj Aziz) recommended merger + 3% NFC share for development + FATA Development Authority → endorsed by 3,000+ elders consulted.
- Promised PKR 110 billion for development "largely unaccounted for" (academia.edu).
- Post-merger assessments: weak/skeletal formal justice; hybrid legal orders coexist (FCR mentality persists including joint liability 40 FCR); Levies/Khasadar→police transition "flawed" with low trust; devolution to local government not implemented; administrative capacity gaps; delayed disbursement of funds; Jirga/malik influence disrupted; land/mineral ownership disputes (China & US interest in minerals).
- 2025 study: 5 merged districts among weakest in health (skilled birth attendance, road accessibility) vs national averages.
- ASPIRE-KP symposium (2026): deficiencies in funding, preparedness, posting of capable personnel, community engagement; security volatility resurgent TTP; lack of comprehensive security plan; inadequate police resources.
- KP government: merged districts = "Newly Merged Districts" (NMDs); 7 districts: Bajaur, Mohmand, Khyber, Orakzai, Kurram, North Waziristan, South Waziristan (plus 6 Frontier Regions absorbed into adjacent districts).
- 3% NFC share: from 2022-23 (?); still contested; funds routed through federal PSDP — need verification.
- TDP/RDP: Return of TDPs (2015-2017); ~4.7m displaced at peak; RDP implemented by KP government + FDMA.

## J. CIVIL SOCIETY, MEDIA, JIRGA (Chapter 12)
- Jirga successes: Kurram 2011 truce (~2,000 killed 2007–2011); 14-point accord Jan 2025; *Aman Teega* May 2025; Swat de-escalation episodes; Tirah return negotiations.
- Jirga critiques: exclusion of women/youth; punitive customary verdicts (swara/nazrana); no enforcement against armed actors; capture by political/military actors; legal pluralism vs statutory criminal law.
- Intermediary collapse: hundreds of maliks/elders assassinated (2004–2014); allowances/patronage removed by merger; peace-committee figures dependent on state patronage.
- Media: KP among world's most dangerous for journalists; PECA/ATA prosecutions; over 1.3m URLs blocked by 2023 (over-broad).
- Women's orgs: Aurat Foundation, Khwendo Kor, Aware Girls (leadership relocated); UN Women's 228-participant Afghan–Pakistani women peacebuilder network.
- Business: chambers (Peshawar, Swat, D.I. Khan, Mardan); extortion/protection payments documented; freelancing/IT as growth sector.

## L. SCENARIOS, TIPPING POINTS, EARLY WARNING (Chapter 14)
- Scenario probabilities (this study's assessments): Sustained normalization 15–20%; Fragile equilibrium 50–60%; Deterioration 25–30%.
- Tipping points (negative): post-2028 expiry without successor; police losses >122 per half-year; TTP taxation for 2+ consecutive quarters in a district; sustained border war; sectarian mass-casualty event; ATC collapse.
- Climate: NDMA forecast 22–26% above-normal monsoon rainfall 2026; 2025 floods KP: 504 deaths, 1.57m affected, ~100,000 displaced, 437 schools, 60 health facilities; 7,000+ glaciers nationally.
- Demography: KP pop ~40m, ~50% under 25; labour force growth ~400,000–600,000/yr vs formal job creation (fraction).
- Technology: Peshawar Safe City 711 AI cameras + facial recognition (D.I. Khan, Bannu, Lakki Marwat in progress); 341 drone attacks foiled H1 2026; no general data-protection statute.

## M. ROADMAP COSTS AND INDICATORS (Chapter 15)
- Merged-district arrears: Rs 568bn; annual obligation under 3% framework ~Rs 192bn (not provided in FY2026-27 budget).
- Police protection package: Rs 50–80bn one-off; Rs 30–50bn/yr recurrent; insurance/compensation Rs 5–10bn/yr.
- Justice expansion Rs 5–10bn/yr; reintegration Rs 10–20bn/yr; NMD education emergency Rs 40–60bn/yr; local government + M&E Rs 5–10bn/yr.
- Aggregate incremental requirement: Rs 150–250bn/yr at maturity (excl. arrears and one-off protection package).
- Ratio: incremental cost = 1/4 to 1/2 of estimated annual conflict loss (Rs 400–800bn).
- End-state targets (2035): ≤400 fatalities/yr; zero districts under TTP taxation for 4 consecutive quarters; ≤50 police killed/yr; ≥75% ATC cases concluded <24 months; transfer delivered & audited; councils elected in all 7 NMDs; NMD poverty within 10 points of provincial average; zero proscriptions of non-violent parties.
- Recommendations matrix: 46 consolidated items with phase (I/II/III), lead institution, indicative cost, KPI.
